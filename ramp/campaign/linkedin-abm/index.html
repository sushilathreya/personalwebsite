The premise

“We can save your company money” asks the reader to do the translation. Ramp’s account-specific LinkedIn ads did some of it for them. A named company, a recognizable spending problem and a relevant next step made a broad finance pitch feel addressed to one buyer.

How it launched

Ramp ran variants around hidden spend, locations and AI readiness. The DraftKings hidden-spend creative and Tennessee account creative show how the account changes the opening. The AI-readiness ads offered another reason to click: assess a problem before discussing a purchase.

The destination mattered as much as the ad. Maestro found both company-specific copy and generic ads that led to account-addressed pages, including demo pages naming a representative. Personalization can carry through the handoff to sales; putting a company name in an image is only the first step.

The same study found a weaker handoff in the savings ads: hidden-spend copy led to a generic email form. Naming the account gets attention. The destination still has to fulfill the promise. Study the continuity of the offer, as well as the personalization.
