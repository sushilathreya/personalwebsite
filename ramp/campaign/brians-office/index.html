The premise

Ramp went looking for the world’s most famous accountant and found him at Dunder Mifflin. Brian Baumgartner, who played Kevin Malone on The Office, would sit in a glass box in Flatiron Plaza and do expense reports by hand. His opponent was Ramp, which said it automated 600,000 receipts a day. Casting Kevin made the setup instantly legible. Watching the pile grow made the product pitch just as easy to understand.

How it launched

Ramp teased Brian’s first day as CFO with a short film and billboards, then declared October 15 National Expense Report Day. The billboards got a bonus punchline: Andy Buckley, who played CFO David Wallace on The Office, spotted one and sent Brian a video insisting he was the real CFO. Ramp brought him into the stunt.

The livestream had a scoreboard and a rotating cast of interruptions. Brian gave the Rizzler life advice, fired two interns, and hosted an actual wedding. Each interruption gave people a reason to tune in or share a clip. The scoreboard brought them back to the same joke: Brian was still filing receipts.

Activation and supporting research figures

  • Ramp’s recap reports 123 receipts manually processed by Brian against the 600,000 challenge benchmark. Ramp’s launch post describes 600,000 as its daily automation volume. Ramp LinkedIn recap, Ramp launch post
  • The Harris Poll surveyed 788 employed U.S. adults who file expense reports on Oct 2–6, 2025. Ramp reported 67% had lost money because of missed deadlines or abandoned submissions, 68% had lost receipts before submission, 33% spent at least two hours monthly filing and 15% spent at least four.
